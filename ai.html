AI and data security

How a company keeps its data safe while using AI.

The risk is rarely the AI itself. It is what people paste into it, what it is allowed to reach, and whether anyone checks what comes out. Four published frameworks cover most of what a company needs.

FrameworkWhat it isUse it for
NIST AI Risk Management Framework, with its Generative AI Profile[11]A US government framework organised around four functions: govern, map, measure and manageStructuring a company-wide AI risk programme and briefing executives and auditors
ISO/IEC 42001[6]The international standard for an AI management system, certifiable like ISO 27001Proving to a parent company or customer that AI is managed systematically
OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications (2025)[12][13]The ten most serious security risks in applications built on language models; prompt injection is first and sensitive information disclosure secondChecking any AI tool or agent the company builds or buys
Philippine NPC guidelines on AI systems processing personal data[14]The privacy regulator's December 2024 advisory on applying the Data Privacy Act to AI systemsAny AI use in the Philippines that touches personal data, such as attendance or contacts
1

Know your data

Classify information before deciding what AI may see: public, internal, confidential, restricted. Personal data and commercial terms sit at the top.

2

Approve the tools

Keep a short list of approved AI tools. Anything else goes through an assessment before use. This is already MOL's rule.[15]

3

Limit what AI can reach

An AI assistant sees whatever its user can open. Tidy up over-shared folders and apply sensitivity labels before switching it on.

4

Keep a person in charge

AI drafts; people decide. Nothing AI writes is sent, filed or acted on without someone checking it.

5

Guard against manipulation

Text hidden in a document or web page can instruct an AI tool. Give agents the least access they need and never let them act on outside content unchecked.[12]

6

Log and review

Record which tools are used, by whom and for what. Review the log, and any incident, on a fixed schedule.

7

Contract for it

Vendors commit in writing: no training on your data, where it is processed, how long it is kept, and breach notice times.

8

Train everyone

Most leaks are honest mistakes. A short rule set that people remember does more than a long policy.

The rule staff remember: red, amber, green

Green · go ahead

  • Public information
  • Your own writing, to improve wording
  • General questions and learning
  • Anything, inside an approved company tool, that you are already allowed to open

Amber · approved tools only

  • Internal reports and meeting notes
  • Vessel performance data
  • Supplier and partner correspondence
  • Attendance and vehicle records

Red · never without sign-off

  • Personal data of seafarers, staff or contacts in any unapproved tool
  • Commercial terms, charter rates and contracts
  • Passwords, keys and system configurations
  • Executive travel and anything marked confidential by headquarters

The three lists are a starting point for discussion. MOL's own classification and policy take precedence.
