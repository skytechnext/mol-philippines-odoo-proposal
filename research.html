What MOL Enterprise Philippines asked for, and what we found out.

Working notes behind the proposal: the key asks from the discovery session of 26 November 2026, research into a MOL-branded business card app, ESP32-based OBD devices and ZKTeco integration, and the points still to confirm.

From the discovery session

Their key asks

In the order the client ranked them. Quotes are from the meeting transcript, lightly tidied.

Priority 1

Business card management

Scan physical cards on a smartphone into a central contact database, mainly for the president and the executive assistant. Integrations asked about: Microsoft, email, WhatsApp, Viber, Apple contacts.

"Having scanned the business cards and then save it to the database quickly — that is the main feature we are looking for."
Priority 2

Driver attendance

For drivers only. They are agency-hired and do not report to the office. A fixed tablet acts as the clock; the phone is for status changes such as leave.

"We want to fix the device, like a biometric for the drivers, since the drivers need not report to our office."
Priority 3

Fleet management with usage monitoring

Track vehicle usage and fuel, and above all how long the engine idles while the vehicle stands. Fuel data today comes from Shell fleet card exports, reported in Power BI.

"How we are going to detect the time spent with the engine idle or moving, so we can minimise the cost."
Constraint

No route tracking

Vehicles carry executives, so trips are confidential. They want driver behaviour, not passenger routes.

"We don't want to track the route of each passenger… there are a lot of confidential business trips."
Ask

One vendor, one environment

The current fleet vendor is not responsive. They want to centralise tools instead of managing many vendors.

"I would like to centralise the tools that we are going to use, instead of having a lot of vendors."
Ask

Proposal fast, training itemised

Proposal and pricing by the next morning, for the accounting budget review that week. Training shown as its own line so it can be isolated.

Ask

Compliance pack for Tokyo

Headquarters runs a vendor assessment covering cybersecurity, safety and compliance. They asked for an SLA, NDA and a draft Master Service Agreement with the proposal.

Later phase

Asset management, built by their team

Tracking, registration, life cycle and a depreciation dashboard for accounting. Their IT team will configure it; they asked for a demonstration.

Also raised

ZKTeco biometric devices

Can the office's existing ZKTeco terminals feed attendance into Odoo?

Also raised

AI and IoT training workshops

Interest in workshops to support automation. External AI tools need a separate security assessment; they will share their AI policy.

Research · vehicle monitoring

ESP32-based OBD devices that send data to the cloud

The question: can a small device in each vehicle's diagnostic port tell Odoo how long the engine was moving, idling or off — without recording where the vehicle went?

What the device must read

Two standard OBD-II values are enough: engine speed (RPM) and vehicle speed. Engine running with speed at zero is idling. Engine run time, fuel level and odometer are useful extras where the vehicle provides them.

Why ESP32

The ESP32 is a low-cost microcontroller with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth built in and a CAN interface for talking to the vehicle. It is programmable, so the firmware can be written to send only what MOL allows.

Why it suits the privacy rule

Commercial trackers are built around GPS. A programmable device can leave GPS out entirely and report engine state and minutes only, which matches the "no routes" requirement.

Risks

What could stop it working

  • Toyota access limits. Newer Toyotas have a security gateway. Standard readings such as RPM and speed are the most likely to remain available, but we found no confirmation for the Alphard, Innova or Zenix. This must be tested on the actual vehicles.
  • Diesel models. The mu-X and Montero may expose fewer standard values. Also to be tested.
  • Battery drain. The device draws power from the port; it must sleep when the engine is off.
  • Warranty and dealer view. Check with the dealers before leaving a device plugged in.
  • Security review. The device, its firmware and the cloud receiver are a new system for the Tokyo assessment.
Recommendation

Pilot before purchase

  • Buy two off-the-shelf ESP32 units (WiCAN Pro and Freematics ONE+).
  • Test on one vehicle of each model for two weeks.
  • Record which values each vehicle exposes and whether idle minutes match a manual log.
  • Send engine state only; no location.
  • Decide then between an off-the-shelf unit and a custom build.
  • Fallback if a model blocks the port: tablet duty records plus Shell fuel data, which need no vehicle hardware.

Preparation · driver attendance

Clock-in with odometer photo and location

The flow

Start and end of day, in the Odoo app: clock in or out with location on, photograph the odometer, type the reading. The reading posts to the vehicle's odometer log in Fleet; the photo is kept as evidence.

What is standard, what is added

Odoo Attendances already supports mobile check-in and can record the location of each one. The odometer photo and reading, and the link from the attendance to the driver's vehicle, are additions we would build.

Points to settle

  • Location at clock-in and clock-out only, never continuous — to state in the driver policy
  • Personal or company phones for agency drivers
  • What happens with no signal: allow a late sync
  • Typed reading is checked against the last one; the photo settles disputes

Third-party examples of location-stamped attendance: v3c_hr_attendance, ps_geofencing_attendance. One Odoo forum user reported inaccurate mobile locations on Odoo 17, so accuracy should be tested on the drivers' phones.

Research · office attendance

Connecting ZKTeco terminals to Odoo

Odoo has no official ZKTeco connector, and we found none maintained by the Odoo Community Association. Several third-party modules exist for current Odoo versions. They connect in one of two ways.

Preferred

Push (ADMS)

The terminal sends each punch to Odoo over the internet as it happens. Nothing needs to reach into the office network, which suits a cloud-hosted Odoo. The terminal's firmware must support ADMS.

Alternative

Pull (pyzk)

Odoo connects to the terminal on port 4370 and downloads the log on a schedule. Odoo and the terminal must be on the same network, or the port must be opened through the office firewall.

Recommendation

Push connector, tested on MOL's devices

Shortlist a push-capable module with reviewable code (DPS or Mindscript), then test it against MOL's actual terminal models before committing. Vendor claims above are unverified.

Hosting matters

Needs Odoo.sh or on-premise

Third-party modules such as these cannot be installed on Odoo Online. The connector requires Odoo.sh or a self-hosted server, which affects the hosting line in the quotation.

Shipping terms in MOL's business

A working glossary. Note that in shipping "fleet" means vessels; in this proposal it always means company vehicles.

Operations

Fleet

To a shipping company, the fleet is its vessels.

Operations

Vessel

The trade word for a ship.

Business line

Dry bulk / bulk carrier

Ships carrying unpackaged cargo such as iron ore, coal, grain and wood chips. MOL Drybulk has an operations hub in the Philippines.

Business line

Car carrier (PCTC / RoRo)

Pure Car and Truck Carriers: vehicles are driven on and off ("roll-on/roll-off"). MOL's are the "ACE" ships.

Business line

LNG carrier

Tanker for liquefied natural gas. MOL states it operates the world's largest LNG carrier fleet.

Business line

FSRU / FLNG

Floating Storage and Regasification Unit; Floating LNG production. Ships that act as offshore gas terminals.

Business line

Chemical tanker

Tanker with many separate tanks for liquid chemicals. A listed MOL business in the region.

Business line

Container shipping / liner

Scheduled services carrying containers. MOL's container business runs through Ocean Network Express (ONE), a joint venture.

Business line

Logistics / cold chain

Land-side transport and warehousing; cold chain means temperature-controlled storage and delivery. MOL Logistics is a regional business.

Operations

Ship management

Running a vessel day to day for its owner: technical upkeep, crewing, compliance.

Operations

Voyage

One trip from loading port to discharge port. Their dry bulk team handles 160+ a year.

Operations

Charter (time / voyage)

Hiring a ship: by period (time charter) or by trip (voyage charter). The hirer is the charterer.

Operations

Port call

A vessel's stop at a port. Staff and agents attend to it.

Operations

Ship agent / husbandry

The local representative who arranges a vessel's needs in port: berthing, supplies, crew changes.

Operations

Bunker

Ship fuel. Usually the largest running cost of a voyage.

Operations

Demurrage / laytime

Laytime is the agreed time to load or unload; demurrage is the charge when it is exceeded.

Operations

Dry-docking / off-hire

Taking a ship out of the water for scheduled maintenance; off-hire is time it cannot earn.

Operations

Vessel performance monitoring

Collecting and cleaning ship data — speed, fuel use — to improve efficiency. EcoMOL does this for about 700 vessels.

Seafarer / crew

People who work on board.

Office employees versus crew on vessels. They are managed and paid differently.
